# Reactive environment prototypes — owner review, 22 September 2026

**Production hand proximity observations do not perceive moving obstacles today.** Adding moving visual geometry alone would fail. The prototype is deliberately separate: geometry, scripted trajectories, renders, and analytic query checks, with no training, policy loading, bank construction, or checkpoint changes.

Open [the visual gallery](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/index.html), [sampling coverage](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/coverage.png), [scene specifications](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/scenes.json), and [measured audit](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/audit.json). Regenerate with `.venv-mjlab/bin/python scripts/prototype_reactive_environments.py` from the repository root. It uses EGL rendering and CPU computations. The script stops below 512 MiB free or above a 55 MiB artifact budget. Videos stream directly to MP4; no frame directories or bank-sized arrays are written.

## What exists, and what the pictures mean

246 proposal instances: 120 E1, 120 E2, three E3 corridors, three E4 crossers. E1/E2 each contain 12 samples per approach direction, 30 per shape, 40 per target choice, and exactly 36 designated near-misses (30%). E2 has 40 each walking, turning, and side-stepping. This is balanced marginal coverage, **not all combinations**. Seed: 20260922. Speeds are sampled uniformly over 0.05–0.50 m/s; target-hand initial center distance is 1.0–1.5 m. Both-hand episodes use two objects, one per hand. Bodies follow predetermined trajectories and do not chase a retreating hand.

Each class has top/side plots of three instances, plus a MuJoCo mesh preview. Plots show actual G1 start-pose skeletons, obstacle projection bounds, trajectory arrows, sizes, speeds, and start heading. Sizes are half-extents, or radius and half-length, in metres. Thin rods have radius 8 mm and full length 44 cm in E1/E2. The E4 rod is 70 cm long. Mesh images show real MuJoCo geometry. **All robot poses/motion are illustrative kinematics, not learned avoidance or dynamically stable walking.** Orange-object overlap is allowed in these visual demonstrations; there are no physical obstacle forces. E1/E4 videos demonstrate approaches/crossings, not successful robot responses.

The approved hand spheres measured in this start pose have centers approximately `(−0.0054, ±0.2735, 0.6112)` m and radius **0.10345 m** ([audit](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/audit.json)). E3 hand-height tips at 0.20/0.35/0.65 m therefore have nominal lateral gaps approximately **−17.7/−2.7/+27.3 cm** when aligned with a hand. The middle setting is borderline/intersecting, not safely outside the 20 cm penalty. Heights and longitudinal locations change actual 3D clearance; these lateral calculations are not route certificates. Shelves and brackets are simple rectangular proxies; pipes are cylinders.

**Below is physically inconsistent with the requested spawn radius.** All 24 below-origin proposals start through/below the floor with this 0.611 m hand height. They are explicitly flagged `requires_below_floor`; the figures mark them infeasible. Keep these as design evidence, not reset candidates. For a real floor scene, start a rising object 0.25–0.40 m below the hand, or use raised hands/platform geometry with independently certified leg/torso clearance. Do not introduce underground threats or teleport through the floor. Object size further reduces usable below-hand range.

Near-miss clearances use **surface-to-hand-envelope distance**, not center distance. The script samples the nominal two-hand trajectory at 301 times per object and saves its minimum; check these as negatives. Measured sampled minimum ranges were **25.47–39.84 cm (E1)** and **25.47–39.74 cm (E2)** ([validation](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/geometry-validation.json)). This does not certify continuous clearance or clearance from torso/head/elbows. A hand miss that hits the chest is not a no-reaction negative. Full-body rejection sampling, reachable escape paths, multiple start arm postures, and floor feasibility remain production admission work. Even direct threats may reach another body region before the intended hand, especially from the opposite side or in both-hand episodes.

## Moving-object infrastructure: concrete integration boundary

Evidence in this working tree:

- `cat_mjlab/scene_bank.py:32` loads `sdf.npy`, `bf.npy`, `gf.npy` into shared tensors; `SceneBank.sample` at line 136 samples them by scene ID. There is no per-environment time/object pose input. `cat_mjlab/fields.py:7` only interpolates those arrays.
- `cat_mjlab/task.py:239` queries these static fields; line 242 subtracts hand envelope radii. The true and delayed actor queries both run at lines 682 and 686. Adding a rendered MuJoCo geom changes neither query.
- `cat_mjlab/task_math.py:96` packs the field channels (hands are positions 5:7). Lines 116–117 pack actor guidance, normals gated by distance <0.5 m, and distance clipped to [−1, 0.5] m. These are the channels corresponding to `pf.hands.df.*.distance` and `pf.hands.{gf,bf}.*` in the original field naming.
- `cat_mjlab/task.py:699` zeroes guidance when the move gate is off. **E1 must rely on distance and obstacle normal, not nonzero standing guidance.** A spawn 1.5 m away is not individually visible in the clipped local hand distance at that range.
- `scripts/diagnose_reactive_clearance_cpu.py:58` replaces `bank.sample` with analytic moving-sphere distances/normals and projected guidance. This is why the earlier diagnostic could see a moving object. Its recorded retreat numbers are user-supplied prior diagnostic results, not new measurements in this review; no policy diagnostic was rerun.
- `cat_mjlab/task_math.py:128` implements the existing 0.20 m anticipation and 0.04 m near-clearance penalty. Preserve it initially; isolate distribution changes from reward changes.

Recommended implementation is a **hybrid query**, keeping the static field immutable:

1. Store a small fixed-capacity object list per environment: shape, size, world pose, trajectory parameters, velocity, validity, and episode clock. Use environment IDs, not scene IDs: two resets sharing a static scene can have different object phases. Advance objects at the simulator substep, reset deterministically, and include state in resume/replay contracts.
2. At every queried body point compute exact primitive point SDFs, take `min(static_sdf, dynamic_sdf)`, then subtract that query's body radius exactly once. Select the corresponding obstacle normal for `bf`; handle ties stably. The prototype checks spheres, axis-aligned boxes, capped cylinders and rods. Production needs local-frame transforms for rotated objects, analytic normals rather than finite differences, masks, and chunked/fused batching.
3. Project local guidance away from the active inward normal near the obstacle, while preserving global static navigation and the explicit locomotion command. This is a **local guidance approximation**, not a recomputed global FMM field. Multiple simultaneously active obstacles can require more than nearest-normal projection. Route around/wait for a blocked moving object explicitly; do not claim the static route is dynamically certified.
4. Apply the same object-time/frame contract to true and actor queries, hands and elbows (`task.py:267`), reward, termination, collision region logging, and evaluation. Existing delayed odometry is updated every five control steps (`task.py:683`); sampling an old robot transform against an inconsistent object timestamp creates false relative motion. Specify sensor latency and retain object pose history or consistently transform current world geometry.
5. Extend dynamic broad-phase/full-body checks. The current static candidate lookup is `cat_mjlab/collision.py:85`; it will not discover newly moving geometry by itself. `task.py:656` performs ten physics substeps and latches collision regions; add moving-object checks at that cadence or use conservative swept tests. At 0.50 m/s the obstacle moves 1 cm/control tick and 1 mm/physics substep, before adding hand speed. A 16 mm rod requires swept reasoning; control-rate endpoint checks can miss crossings. Virtual collision/termination preserves the existing obstacle semantics (`cat_mjlab/model.py:14`, `generalist_fields.py:296`). Physical pushing/contact would be a separate, more expensive simulation feature.

After these changes distance/normal inputs can perceive moving geometry **without increasing input dimensions**. Until then, E1/E2/E4 are unusable as training scenes. Identical current distance/normal values cannot distinguish approaching, receding, and stationary objects. For E4 timing, add obstacle relative velocity/time-to-contact or a short observation history and test aliasing; this changes the observation/checkpoint contract unless an existing temporal mechanism is explicitly introduced. Pure turning needs command work too: `task_math.py:113` zeroes delayed command's final component, and `task.py:254` builds room commands with zero yaw. Do not promise that today's command path already supports E2 turning in place.

## Cost and thin-object representation

[Grid arithmetic](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/grid-cost.json) reads the current v6 bank metadata only: 2,374 scenes at 4 cm, median 142,500 voxels, maximum 4,318,920. At 30,000 environments, the median grid duplicated per environment needs **119.7 GB** for seven float32 channels, or **17.1 GB** for SDF alone. A complete 50 Hz rewrite is **5.985 TB/s** for seven channels or **0.855 TB/s** for SDF alone, excluding occupancy, read traffic, gradients, temporary buffers, EDT/FMM computation and physics. Existing offline generation invokes SDF, gradient, and progressive guidance (`cat_ppo/furniture/generalist_fields.py:253`, lines 281–285). **Do not rebuild these dense per-environment fields each step at 30k.** Local sparse updates are theoretically possible but would be new infrastructure and would not cheaply refresh global guidance.

For 30k environments × 11 points × four objects, a hybrid pass is **1.32 million point/primitive pairs**, linear in object count. A float32 XYZ intermediate is 15.84 MB; four-object state at 16 float32 values/object is 7.68 MB. True and delayed observations approximately double query work; elbow queries add more. Body collision at 35 proxies × four objects × 30k × ten substeps is **42 million proxy/object pairs per control tick** before broad-phase pruning. Restricting dynamics to the proposed 28% E1+E2+E4 environments reduces that nominal dynamic workload to 11.76 million pairs. Complex box/capsule collision costs more than point-to-sphere queries.

The [audit](assets/reactive-prototypes-20260922/audit.json) records a **measured two-thread CPU Torch eager benchmark: median 12.79 ms, range 12.61–13.13 ms**, 10 timed repetitions after three warmups, for the 1.32-million-pair sphere distance + nearest-normal query. It excludes bank lookup, guidance, physics and collision checks. This is not a GPU measurement or complete environment step benchmark. The renderer does not benchmark compute kernels.

**Planning estimate, unmeasured:** a fused GPU point query moving 30–100 MB at an assumed effective 300–1,000 GB/s has a memory-only floor of roughly 0.03–0.33 ms/pass. Allocation, reductions, shape branches, kernel launch overhead and actual hardware can dominate. Budget approximately **1–5 ms/control tick for hybrid observation work**, and **2–20 ms additional collision work** for a naive all-environment four-object implementation; these are broad engineering allowances, not a performance promise. Measure fused versus eager, active-subset versus full-batch, one/two/four objects, and full substep collision before admitting 30k. Report incremental time and peak memory against the actual baseline; no overall slowdown percentage can be inferred without that baseline.

The default field resolution is **4 cm** (`generalist_fields.py:253`). A **16 mm diameter rod is sub-voxel**: it cannot be represented with faithful shape or millimetre surface clearance on that grid. Conservative box-cell intersection (`room_geometry.py:26`) avoids dropping a *box enclosure* of it, but overcovers geometry by up to one voxel diagonal (~6.93 cm at 4 cm); that bound is for the occupied-cell union, not a guarantee for interpolated runtime SDF. Center-only occupancy can miss thin objects entirely. Cylinders are not native room box primitives. Use exact cylinder/capsule SDFs for rods and collision checks; don't quietly inflate rods and call that accurate. The existing hand sphere is also a 10.3 cm envelope, not individual fingers. Thin-rod success at that envelope does not demonstrate finger-level safety. Prototype analytic check: a point beside the rod changes from **0.292 m to 0.092 m** after a 0.20 m translation, with the correct +Y normal (point distance, before hand-radius subtraction).

## Effort, format and sequencing

Estimates below are engineer-days for one person familiar with this code, after design agreement. They include integration checks, not training, policy success, or physical-contact support. Allow roughly 50% contingency for observation/command compatibility and collision performance surprises.

| Work | Incremental effort | Main risk / format |
|---|---:|---|
| Shared moving-primitive engine | 5–9 days | Reset/time/delay consistency, query fusion, substep collision, deterministic replay and parity tests. New versioned dynamic episode specification referencing an immutable static bank. |
| E1 | 2–4 days after engine | Floor/body feasibility, reachable retreat, stationarity command, calibrated negatives and no-hunch metrics. New trajectory metadata; same static floor background. |
| E2 | 3–6 days after E1 | Moving interception feasibility, turn command/gait contract, coordinate frames, body motion and actor observability. Shares E1 schema. |
| E3 | 2–4 days independently | Static full-body/route/reset certification; prevent trivial sidestep or torso collision. Boxes can use existing room format; pipes need conservative box approximations or extended geometry support. |
| E4 | 3–6 days for translation after engine/E2; another 3–6 for swing and timing observations | Partial observability, waiting/stalling exploits, phase coverage, swept collision and changing checkpoint inputs. Same dynamic schema plus pivot/phase parameters; no dense time-indexed banks. |

**Do E1–E3 first, defer E4 training.** E4 geometry is cheap once E1's engine exists; E4's incremental *behaviour and validation* are not. Its 3% allocation is unlikely to justify a separate timing objective before standing retraction works. Keep the E4 visual prototype to review future scope. E3 is immediately compatible with today's static architecture after certification, but it cannot close the stationary-reactivity gap alone.

## Mixture and review gates

Your proposed percentages sum to 100. The new classes total **35%**, but moving/reactive E1+E2+E4 total **28%**; E3's 7% is static. Calling all 35% reactive hides this distinction.

Start at **75% retention / 25% new**, with standing threats the largest new component. Later try 65/35, reallocating E4's 3% to E3 until timing is explicitly justified. There is no evidence that 65% guarantees retention. User-provided working scores (clutter 0.72, narrow 0.58, CAT 0.14) should be treated as baseline reference values, not new results or directly comparable objective scales. CAT's low score particularly argues against assuming it is robust.

Before future training, freeze a held-out evaluation distribution and checkpoint baseline. Use paired seeds and binomial/paired uncertainty appropriate to the actual metric. A provisional review gate could be no more than **0.03 absolute** regression on each stated metric (0.69/0.55/0.11), with uncertainty reported and stricter CAT treatment if that drop is unacceptable; this tolerance is a proposal, not statistically established. Evaluate stationary drift/falls, per-direction hand clearance/contact, time inside 20 cm, unnecessary hand displacement on negatives, recovery to nominal posture, moving progress and waiting duration. Do not approve an aggregate gain that hides catastrophic below/back or CAT regressions. Balance gradient exposure by sampled transitions as well as reset count: at 5 cm/s, a 1–1.5 m approach takes 20–30 s; at 50 cm/s, 2–3 s. Episode-mass percentages do not imply equal threat-state training mass.

**30% near-misses is a sensible starting hypothesis, not a calibrated optimum.** Keep 30% of E1/E2 with 25–40 cm *minimum surface clearance*, and include matched no-object/receding controls plus post-threat empty recovery within episodes. Randomize arrival time and starting posture so a permanently tucked posture is measurable and not rewarded by episode structure. At your mix, E1/E2 misses account for 7.5% of all resets; they are not 30% of the whole curriculum. Compare 20/30/40% negatives only after the perception/admission checks pass. Continuous posture/effort penalties and recovery measurements are still needed; negative geometry alone does not punish hunching.

The most important missing condition is **self-motion into a static obstacle while starting close**: turning beside a shelf, backing up, lateral motion, and raising/lowering a hand near an underside. It provides rear/side/below relative motion without new moving-object infrastructure, and is likely a better early addition than E4. Add matched approaching/receding/dwell/recovery episodes and a small feasible both-hand subset; never demand a simultaneous retreat for which no whole-body escape exists. These are especially valuable given the strong front/weak side/no-below response described in the request.

This package validates proposal geometry and visibility arithmetic only. It does not certify resets, production observation integration, full-body safety, collision throughput, stable locomotion, or learned avoidance.
