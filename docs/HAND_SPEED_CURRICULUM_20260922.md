## Evidence and diagnosis

The speed-first hypothesis is reasonable: maintaining the required hand position while moving calls for a different whole-body support strategy. A lack of falls alone does not show that the policy has learned that strategy. Dropping the hands while continuing to walk is compatible with an objective tradeoff. The specific explanation that lost arm swing/angular-momentum cancellation causes the tradeoff remains an inference, not a directly measured causal result.

Two important limits emerged from reading the actual sources:

- The recorded 4800/4800 compliant standing samples, 9.62 s streak, 100% two-foot contact and 0.10 s loss after switching to walking are real. However, the experiment used **v3 geometry**, analytic leg feedback for successful standing, a fixed upper preset and disabled randomization. The zero-command incumbent itself scored 0/4800 after step 100. See `outputs/raised_dynamic_hold_20260921/REPORT.md:5`, `:14`, `:21`, `:27`, `:31`. Standing feasibility is established for that controller/geometry; current v5 learned standing is not yet established. The curriculum does not install that analytic controller or freeze the arms: the policy must learn the leg/arm strategy under the active requirement.
- The v5 pilot reproduces root-height p50 .758341 -> .770629 m and route-speed .397539 -> .761159 m/s. Those `balance/policy_*` metrics are **flat-only**, because `cat_mjlab/balance.py:47` masks by `balance/flat`. They cannot establish protected-zone stability. Its flat fall counters sum to 69 across 50 updates, so “zero falls throughout the whole pilot” is not supported by that counter. This does not establish falls in protected scenes either. The user-reported protected-population fall rate is not independently verified here. See [speed-checks.json](assets/hand-tolerance-v6-20260922/speed-checks.json).

These qualifications do not change the implementation priority. They limit the claims about what the speed curriculum is already known to solve. No learning effectiveness or GPU performance result is claimed.

## Ladder, scope and anti-degeneracy

Exact rungs: **0, .2, .3, .45, .6 m/s** (`cat_mjlab/speed_curriculum.py:9`). Zero isolates pose acquisition/support from stepping. .2 establishes slow forward movement; .3 is a smaller first walking increment, followed by .15 increments to the original .6 command. These are engineering choices, not empirically proven feasible intermediate speeds.

The cap applies only to forward-protected or transition roles, inside a declared hand-active zone with a valid region and positive hand phase weight (`speed_curriculum.py:59`). It excludes incoming approach-only shaping, flat, CAT, clutter, standalone narrow scenes, and the hand-inactive narrow module inside transition scenes. It is a **command cap**, not a physical velocity clamp; existing goal taper/blocked-route stops remain effective. Actual movement is checked separately. Both true/delayed actor commands and root tracking rewards see the capped command (`cat_mjlab/task.py:240`). Other task rows are unchanged in the CPU field comparison test.

The zero rung is a bounded bootstrap, not a perpetual standing objective:

1. Enter a hand core zone; allow up to **2.5 s acquisition/settling** under the strict, fully active hand requirement. This allowance accounts for the stored standing report's transient and its slower nominal-arm acquisition; it is not a tolerance relaxation.
2. Score the next **2 s**: >=90% of samples must satisfy both strict 5 cm hand compliance and existing heading compliance. Accumulated absolute route motion during that hold must be <=5 cm.
3. Release the command to **.2 m/s**, restoring alternating gait phase, and require >=**10 cm net forward progress within 2 s**, without failure or leaving the trial core. Zero command lasts at most 4.5 s in this episode, regardless of success. After release/failure, all subsequent active hand zones use .2 for the remainder of this zero-rung episode. Hand reward remains active during release, but release compliance is not part of the zero-rung pass: this is intentionally a bootstrap to the first walking rung.

Positive rungs require **2 s**, >=90% strict hand-plus-heading compliance, and >=80% of the commanded two-second net displacement: **.32, .48, .72, .96 m** respectively. That much displacement must also accumulate **while compliant**, preventing a policy from standing compliantly for 90% of the trial and making all progress with dropped hands during the remaining 10%. Net displacement above 120% of commanded distance +2 cm fails the trial, so racing through does not qualify as learning a slower gait. See `speed_curriculum.py:136` and `tests/test_speed_curriculum.py`.

One first-core-zone trial is assigned per episode. Early core exit, collision/fall/other episode failure and resolved episodes with no entry fail closed. This deliberately measures **local hand maintenance with motion**, not clean-goal/full-passage success; the existing strict posture and passage-acceptance metrics remain unchanged. At .6, a two-second nominal trial covers 1.2 m within v6's 1.35 m first-zone core (1.65 m zone minus two .15 m fades). Seeded poses that start too far into the zone can fail the duration requirement; seeded trials are diagnostic and never drive the ladder.

## Advancement, demotion, logs and compatibility

Advance one rung when **both protected and transition** roles reach >=80% successful trials in their latest **256 unseeded leader episodes**. Seeded samples are logged separately, never used for advancement/demotion. Require full windows, including no-entry and physical failures; missing evidence cannot pass. Near 80%, 256 independent trials would have ~2.5 percentage-point binomial standard error, though parallel RL samples are correlated. This is an engineering stability window, not a significance test.

Demote one rung if either role has **two consecutive non-overlapping 256-trial windows below 40%** success. The 80/40 gap and two windows provide hysteresis against single noisy dips. Demotion works from .6 too; rung zero is the floor. The local width implementation only auto-advances (`cat_mjlab/task.py`, width branch in `_outcomes`); its current code does not contain the requested demotion behavior, so this implementation adds it explicitly.

Rung and generation are frozen at reset. A completion from an older generation cannot enter a new window, including after demotion/re-promotion. All windows, counters and in-flight trial fields are preserved by the existing task checkpoint serializer. Fresh native warm starts begin at rung zero. Logs: `hand_speed/rung`, `command_m_s`, promotions/demotions, current per-role window count/success/bad-window streak, and per-rung successes/completed/mean_strict_compliance, plus seeded diagnostic counts. The rung's command log is its nominal speed; zero-rung release is explicitly .2 as documented above.

CLI: `--hand-speed-curriculum`, `--hand-speed-curriculum-window 256`, `--hand-speed-curriculum-success-threshold .8`, `--hand-speed-curriculum-demote-threshold .4`. Omission is a no-op, including ignoring an inherited speed-curriculum config on a fresh launch. Speed and tolerance flags are mutually exclusive at argument parsing, before runtime/GPU import, with a second config-level check.

## Composition and verification

Use **v6 + speed first, strict .05 tolerance, priority weights**, keeping the reset mixture fixed throughout so it is not another changing curriculum variable. I agree with testing one ladder at a time. I would not automatically loosen tolerance after successfully mastering .6 at .05: that would relax an already-achieved local goal. Keep tolerance-only as a separate, attributable follow-up/ablation if strict acquisition stalls or whole-passage generalization remains weak; disable speed curriculum for that run, restoring ordinary .6 route commands. Never equate either ladder's local success with complete passage acceptance. Existing best.pt selection still emphasizes flat/retention; inspect strict unseeded hand and passage metrics rather than assuming best.pt is best for hands.
